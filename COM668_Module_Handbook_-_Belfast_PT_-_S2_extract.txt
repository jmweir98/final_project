3. Aim and Learning Outcomes 
 
 
 
Module Rationale 
The Computing Project provides an opportunity to draw together learning from across the course, and 
to allow students to evidence their mastery of the academic content and of its application through 
professional practice. Through the opportunity to devise, manage and evaluate all aspects of work in 
addressing a significant challenge, students can gain independence and a deeper appreciation of their 
practice within the broader subject area and of its relationship to wider society. These skills and the 
related knowledge are seen as essential by prospective employers and provide a sound basis for further 
study and continued professional development. 
 
Module Aim  
• 
Provide experience of devising and evaluating a solution to significant challenge. 
• 
Integrate and contextualise learning from across the course. 
• 
Develop a deeper appreciation for effective professional practice. 
• 
Reinforce the interplay between professional practice and wider society. 
• 
Foster independence through the opportunity to self-manage a large project. 
• 
Refine skills in communicating complex concepts. 
 
Learning Outcomes 
Successful participants will be able to: 
1) Synthesise and Evaluate Solutions: Synthesize diverse information and ideas to devise a coherent, 
innovative and creative solution to a significant challenge relevant to the specific course of study, 
and critically evaluate the effectiveness of that solution.  
2) Needs, Risks, and Societal Context: Analyze the needs, associated risks, and societal context of 
a challenge and its resulting solution, ensuring these factors are met and managed effectively, 
while verifying and validating both the process and the product.  
3) Methodological Integration and Self-Management: Integrate a range of analytical and practical 
skills within an appropriate methodology, justify the suitability of the chosen process, and actively 
self-manage its delivery, informed by critical self-evaluation. 
4) Professional Communication: Communicate diverse forms of information in a meaningful, justified, 
critical, and professional manner, demonstrating clarity and professionalism in all forms of 
communication.

Assessment Guidance 
1. Word counts include all text in the main body of reports, including  in-text references and citations 
but excludes title page, contents, tables, reference list and appendices.  
Please Note, appendices, tables and diagrams must not include narrative, discussion, analysis, or 
similar writing. They must only include additional items that evidence practice claimed, discussed 
or analysed within the main body of the report. For example, an appendix could include a list of 
detailed user requirements but not a description of the requirements gathering process.  
Important note: Words in excess of +10% of the word count will not be marked. 
2. Your work should include references to relevant journal articles and other good quality information 
sources and should be properly laid out using the Harvard system of referencing. More detailed 
guidance is available in the Subject Guides. We encourage you to make good use of all the support 
services offered by your Campus Librarian, further details are in the Learning Support Services 
Section.  
3. You should refer to the assessment criteria to provide fuller details of the marking criteria for each 
classification band. 
4. In addition, you should refer to the standard assessment guidelines as presented in your 
Programme Handbook/Support Area, this includes guidance and policies on referencing style, 
plagiarism, etc. 
5. We recommend the use of Studiosity which offers constructive feedback on draft written 
submissions within 24 hrs.   
6. Coursework must be submitted by the dates specified. Coursework submitted after the deadline, 
without prior approval, is not normally accepted. For further guidance on the late submission of 
coursework, please see the Programme handbook. 
7. This module is assessed using one staged coursework that consists of four assessment tasks. 
Students must submit meaningful attempts at all assessment tasks to successfully complete the 
module. 
8. Assessments must be submitted as per the assessment requirements stated in this handbook.  
9. The software developed for the assessment must remain in an executable state, unchanged since 
the AT3 demonstration was recorded, and available for inspection until after the module has 
completed, i.e. typically until after you have graduated.

Assessment Task 1 – Concept Proposal (must use form provided, formative) 
The concept proposal task results in a form that should define a software development challenge to be 
addressed during the module. The subject of the proposal can be of your choosing but must be agreed 
with and approved by your Mentor before work commences. A form is provided for this purpose and 
must be used. The project proposal should: briefly describe the proposed concept; define the aim of 
the project; identify any resources required; and identify any intellectual property right concerns. The 
material in this report is not assessed at this time but is required to facilitate agreement of a project 
challenge and early-stage review of the project proposal regarding: ethics; health and safety; intellectual 
property rights; and validation against the BCS accreditation guideline for major projects. The format of 
the form must be in keeping with the form provided and presented in a manner that allows assessment. 
Please see the associated key consideration listed later in this handbook.  
 
Note, this task also includes two follow-on subtasks performed by the Mentor, to gain ethics approval 
for the agreed proposal and to ensure it meets the guidelines set by the BCS. While the Mentor is 
responsible for completing these follow-on tasks, you are expected to afford your Mentor any support 
needed to complete these activities successfully on your behalf. A project cannot proceed without 
agreed completion of all aspects of AT1.  
 
Assessment Task 2 – Challenge Definition Report (3600 words, 45% of module) 
The challenge definition task results in a technical report that should define a software development 
challenge to be addressed during the module. The challenge definition report should: describe the

and similar solutions, with references; justify the selected software development process; define the 
requirements of the project; and assess the project risks. The format of the report must be in keeping 
with the guidance provided and presented in a manner that allows assessment of the work.

Assessment Task 3 – Software Demonstration Video (15 minutes, 25% of module) 
The software demonstration is a video presentation comprising of two parts: a software demonstration 
and a detailed code review. Typically, each part being of equal duration. The presentation should 
provide insight into the aim, scope, and quality of the resulting software product and of the student’s 
detailed understanding and ownership of the underlying source code. The video and audio quality must 
be of a good standard to allow assessment of the work.  
 
Important note: The software developed for the assessment must remain in an executable state, 
unchanged since the AT3 demonstration was recorded, and available for inspection until after the 
module has completed, i.e. until you have graduated.  
 
Assessment Task 3 – Content and Structure 
This is a guide to the typical content and structure to be included in this Assessment. However, you 
may wish to adapt this to accommodate the specific nature of your project and the methodology being 
used. Your mentor will be able to guide you further if this is the case.  
 
 
Important note: You must provide commentary in English using your natural voice. No one else should 
speak on the video and you should not use an artificially generated voice. The only exception is if you 
have an approved Reasonable Adjustment Recommendations that that allows this. Also note that the 
video must clearly display a video recording of you in one corner, where it does not obscure any of the 
material being demonstrated.  
 
 
Video Format:  
• 
Four parts: introduction, software demonstration, detailed source code walkthrough, conclusion. 
•

– On-screen text, such as software menu items and outputs, should be clearly legible,

– Do not speed the recording to fit more in, this shows poor planning and communication, 
– HD resolution is recommended, but take care regarding file size for upload purposes. 
• 
Video file should use a commonly available video format, e.g. MP4.

Report Formatting Guidelines 
The following guidelines for layout and submission should be noted for preparing all reports: 
 
Writing style 
Students should write in a clear technical style, in the third-person, i.e. avoiding use of personal 
pronouns. Care should be taken to check reports for spelling and grammar. Note, any reasonable 
adjustment requests that is in place will be considered in relation this this.  
 
 
 
Figures, tables, lists and Appendices 
It can be helpful to include a few important or representative diagrams, images, tables, and lists in the 
body of the report for direct reference. However, very large or repetitive items should mostly be placed 
in an appendix. For example, you might include some representative requirements, test cases or design

Numbering Convention 
Except for the front page, all the pages before Chapter 1 are numbered with roman numerals, so that 
the page following the front page is ‘ii, and so on’. From the start of chapter 1, decimal numbering is 
then used until to the end of the report, including the reference section and appendices. This means 
that the first page of chapter 1 is page “1” and numbering continues from there.  
 
Chapters are numbered sequentially 1, 2, 3, etc, while appendices are identified alphabetically in 
sequence Appendix A, Appendix B, Appendix C, etc. Chapter, section and sub-sections are numbered 
as in the following outline example:  
Chapter        2. 
Section         2.1. 
Section         2.2. 
Subsection   2.2.1. 
Subsection   2.2.2. 
Section         2.3. 
Note, as a general practice, if you find that your numbering needs to go deeper than three levels, e.g. 
deeper than 2.2.1. in the example above, then you might need to consider restructuring the writing.  
 
Figures, tables, diagrams, code snippets, etc. should be labelled in keeping with their type, numbered 
to indicate the chapter they are within, with sub-numbering  to indicate their sequence. They should 
also have a descriptive title. For example, in chapter 2 this might look as follows:  
Table 2.1, descriptive title goes here 
Table 2.2, descriptive title goes here 
Figure 2.1 descriptive title goes here 
Code snippet 2.1, descriptive title goes here 
Figure 2.2 descriptive title goes here 
Table 2.3, descriptive title goes here 
 
Referenced material and quotations 
Quotations and all material from an external source should be fully attributed in keeping with University 
policy and using the Harvard style (see link below). If you are in any doubt as to what needs referenced 
or how to do so then you must ask your mentor for advice.

Rubrics and Key Considerations 
The rubrics for each of the assessed Assessment Tasks are presented below. These show the criteria 
that each Assessment Task is marked against along with indicative characteristics of how each of these 
criteria can be met, at different levels of attainment. However, the highly individual nature of most 
projects means that these can only act as a guide. Therefore, it is important that you discuss the rubrics 
and the specifics of your project with your Mentor. 
 
Assessment Task 1 Concept proposal, Key consideration when agreeing a proposal 
1. The outcome must comply with BCS expectations: 
• 
It is mandatory that the project meets the stipulated BCS expectations. 
• 
It is more generally wise to comply with the BCS expectations for undergraduate projects. 
2. Addressing a genuine need: 
• 
Check that the proposed project addresses a tangible and relevant need. 
• 
Ensure that the project offers an opportunity to contribute to a computing related problem. 
3. Integration of Course Learning: 
• 
Ensure that the project relates to the knowledge and skills gained during the course of study. 
• 
Check that the project serves as a capping of the student's overall learning experience. 
4. Challenge and Academic Excellence: 
• 
Ensure that the proposed project offers a significant technical challenge. 
• 
Check that an optimal solution would afford the highest level of academic performance. 
5. Risk Assessment: 
• 
Ensure that potential risks to completion of the project have been thoroughly evaluated. 
• 
Check for notable obstacles, e.g. relating to resource availability or stakeholder access.  
• 
Consider ethical consideration to ensure approval is feasible (approval sought separately).

Coursework Feedback 
For AT1, verbal formative feedback will be provided during the proposal agreement process and once 
the agreed proposal has been submitted. Summative feedback, in the form of confirmation that the 
submitted proposal has been received and agreed, ethics approval received, and that the proposal 
meets the BCS guidelines, will be made available on Blackboard.  
For AT2, AT3 and AT4, formative feedback will be provided verbally on an on-going basis, as work is 
presented and discussed during group and individual Mentor meetings. Written summative feedback 
will be made available on Blackboard in keeping with the University’s policy on feedback (see dates in 
the summary assessment information table).
